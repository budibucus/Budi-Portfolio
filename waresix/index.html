The challenge

Waresix had scaled fast — one of Indonesia's largest first-mile logistics SaaS platforms, serving 1,000+ enterprise partners. The internal machinery running that network hadn't scaled with it.

Partner onboarding, contract terms, SLA monitoring, invoice verification — all still largely manual, built for a partner network a fraction of the current size. There was no unified system for order transactions either, or for the multi-party financial settlements between shippers and transporters; every settlement was its own manual reconciliation exercise. Growth was starting to outpace operations.

What I did

Building the tooling operations actually needed

I owned the internal tooling that ran partner operations end to end — onboarding, contract terms, SLA monitoring, invoice verification — with one goal: replace the spreadsheets with something that could scale alongside a fast-growing partner network. SLA monitoring got real exception-handling logic, so breaches and edge cases surfaced automatically instead of being discovered downstream by someone in operations. Invoice verification got streamlined too, stripping out the manual checks that had quietly been slowing down every settlement.

Giving the business a system of record

In parallel, I led requirements and delivery for the order management system — the backbone for shipment transactions and the financial settlements between shippers and transporters. Getting there meant sitting with operations, finance, engineering, and commercial long enough to turn workflows that lived only in people's heads into specifications an engineering team could build against, then managing backlog and sprint planning across both workstreams at once — weighing what operations needed this week against what the platform would need a year from now.

Scaling onboarding without scaling headcount

As the partner network grew past 1,000 enterprise partners, onboarding itself became a bottleneck. Building internal tooling around this process — rather than adding more manual review — was what ultimately cut onboarding time from 1.5 months to under 1 month.

The outcome

Workflow automation lifted operational throughput 15%, cutting the manual verification checks and delays that had built up across the settlement pipeline. Partner onboarding — the bottleneck that kept getting worse as the network passed 1,000 enterprise partners — dropped from 1.5 months to under one, without adding a single person to review it. And for the first time, Waresix had one system handling shipment transactions and multi-party settlements end to end, instead of a patchwork of manual reconciliation.

The takeaway. In an internal-tooling context, the biggest wins often aren't user-facing features — they're removing the manual steps that quietly cap how fast the business can grow. Getting operations, finance, engineering, and commercial aligned on shared system specifications was the real unlock here.
