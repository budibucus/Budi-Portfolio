How I work

Each role has built on the last in a fairly deliberate way. At Home Credit I learned to build inside a regulated fintech environment, where a scoring mistake or a fraud gap has real financial consequences. At Waresix I learned to build for scale, where the hardest problems were operational, not technical. At Geekzwolf I took a real swing at building something of my own — it didn't work, but it taught me more about timing and product-market fit than most of my successes did. At Rey.id all of that came together: AI-powered automation inside a regulated healthcare and insurance platform.

I'm currently exploring senior product roles, and always happy to compare notes on 0→1 product building.

  • I've gotten used to starting from nothing. Most of my roles began with "build something from a blank page" — a marketplace, tooling for a partner network that had outgrown its spreadsheets, an AI pipeline in an industry that doesn't forgive mistakes. I'd rather sit with the problem a while longer than rush to a solution.
  • The number is never the whole story. "$1M in GMV" or "claims cut from two months to three days" only mean something once you know what got tried and abandoned first, and which trade-off actually moved the needle. I try to write and talk about the road there, not just the destination.
  • Compliance and good UX aren't in tension — if you plan for both early. From OJK-regulated fintech to AI running inside regulated healthcare, the projects that went smoothly were the ones where compliance had a seat at the table before engineering started, not after something needed fixing.
