**The promise, and the harsh reality**

The idea was to onboard Southeast Asian gamers to Web3, letting them invest in and earn from their gameplay. The catch: Web3 games were still in their infancy. Most game studios we engaged with were building gaming tokens, not good gameplay — they hadn't cracked how to make games people wanted to play for fun, not just token rewards. We could generate interest, but long-term engagement was hard to sustain when the games themselves weren't keeping players around.

**The token crash**

Then came the second blow: gaming token prices collapsed. Geekzwolf's co-investment platform — where gamers and investors pooled resources to fund gaming guilds — worked well when token prices were high. As prices crashed, the entire co-investing model became irrelevant almost overnight.

**Onboarding Web3 studios to Indonesia: a tough road**

One core strategy was bringing Web3 game studios into Indonesia's gaming market. In practice, most of the studios we worked with were still testing their games — none had something fully developed or market-ready. Their focus was tokenomics over gameplay, so when the token economy collapsed, there was little left to keep players engaged.
