The challenge

By 2023, Rey.id had something most digital-health companies never earn: an established name in Indonesian healthcare and insurance, a real user base, and real trust. The product hadn't caught up to that trust. Doctor consultations and insurance claims were still slow and manual, and the platform was leaving revenue and efficiency on the table that its user base would happily have supported.

I joined as VP of Product to own the full roadmap across three verticals at once — telehealth, insurance, and medical data — none of which could wait for the others. Two years later, back as Product Consultant, the problem had narrowed to a sharp point: claims for a state-owned insurer still took two months each, and Rey.id had no mass-market channel to reach anyone beyond its existing base.

What I did

Redesigning around how people actually behaved

We rebuilt the app's design and flow around consumer research and real behaviour, not assumptions. Navigation to the services people actually used — doctor consultations, mental health support, insurance claims — got simpler, and first-response time on doctor chat dropped to under 5 minutes. Watching the checkout funnel closely turned up the two screens where most people gave up, so we rebuilt KYC document upload and payment error handling around exactly those failure points — cart abandonment fell from 93% to 85%. Wiring the app directly into partner hospital and pharmacy systems for real-time fulfilment did the rest: teleconsultation utilization rose 25%, and getting a prescription filled went from 110 minutes to 75.

Teaching claims to process themselves

The claims process was the most manual thing left in the business — invoices processed by hand, documents reviewed one at a time. I led the project to digitize it, later extending the same system to a state-owned insurer handling 6,000 claims a month across 100K+ members. Working alongside data engineering, we defined exactly what training data structure and validation criteria the models needed to read hospital invoices and documents reliably. None of it could ship without guardrails — regulated healthcare doesn't forgive a confident-but-wrong AI — so escalation logic and human-in-the-loop review went into the workflow from the start, with the models set up to keep learning as they handled more complex claims over time.

Opening a channel that didn't exist before

I owned end-to-end product definition for enterprise client onboarding — translating client policy rules and operational workflows into system requirements — and managed external stakeholders across telco partners, state-owned enterprise clients, and licensed medical practitioners. This included co-creating an embedded insurance product bundled into a major telco's mobile data packages, opening a mass-market distribution channel that hadn't existed for Rey.id before.

Letting data settle the arguments

Every decision was backed by data — funnel instrumentation and analytics (Mixpanel) let the team iterate on real behavioural data rather than assumption, across usage patterns, feedback, and service efficiency.

The outcome

As VP of Product, the platform scaled to 100K+ active users and $1M in GMV within 12 months across telehealth, insurance, and payments. Conversion climbed from 0.01% to 1.5%, the app got 30% faster, and the funnel work held: cart abandonment down from 93% to 85%, teleconsultation use up 25%, prescriptions filled 35 minutes faster.

Back as Product Consultant, the AI claims work landed where it needed to. A state-owned insurer processing 6,000 claims a month across 100K+ members now gets a decision in under 3 days instead of 2 months. And the embedded insurance product bundled into a major telco's mobile packages did what no marketing campaign had — it brought 200,000+ new users through a channel Rey.id had never had access to before.

The takeaway. The two roles told a continuous story — first proving the platform could scale on strong fundamentals (UX, funnel discipline, partner integrations), then applying that same discipline to AI automation in a regulated environment where trust and accuracy couldn't be compromised for speed.
